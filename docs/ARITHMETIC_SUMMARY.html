Arithmetic, not biology

denali showed that set-level rankings are dominated by how the sets were built — chiefly their size — rather than by what they mean. If that is arithmetic, it must appear wherever a score is aggregated over sets of varying size. It does: in six fields that share no biology, including the best-annotated organism in the world. And it is absent from AI benchmarks for one identifiable reason, which tells you exactly what genomics is missing.

The negative first

AI benchmarks are not confounded, and that is the finding

Every LLM benchmark is a set of items of wildly varying size — MMLU's 57 subjects run from 100 to 1,534 items. So the question is whether leaderboard position is partly an artifact of benchmark construction. We pre-registered the expectation that it would not be, and why.

Count layer · items correct
R² 0.598
Confounded for 100% of the panel — harder than denali's own headline of 0.465.
Rate layer · accuracy
ρ 0.12
Clean. Item difficulty does not meaningfully track how many items a subject has.

The whole difference is one division. Accuracy is a rate; a gene-set hit count is a count. Normalising by item count is the entire protection.

We measured the floor rather than asserting it

Replace each model with a hypothetical one scoring its own mean accuracy on every subject — no capability variation at all, so any size dependence left is arithmetic and nothing else. That null scores 0.807. Real models score 0.598 — below the arithmetic floor, for 99.9% of the panel. So the count-layer number says nothing bad about any benchmark; it measures how strong the arithmetic is with nothing normalising it.

A second protection stacks on the first. Item-weighted averaging gives the largest subject 15.3× the weight of the smallest, and the five biggest subjects carry 31% of all items — the construction imbalance is real and large. It does not propagate, because model ability is correlated across subjects at r = 0.87. A model good at one subject is good at the others, so re-weighting cannot reorder much. Set-level genomics has neither protection.

Our own criterion fired, and we are reporting how

The registered rule for "construction moves the leaderboard" tripped: top-5 membership changed, and 93% of models moved ≥ 3 ranks. The verdict stands as registered — a criterion is never revised after seeing data. But the top-5 swap is separated by 0.000071 — exactly one item out of 14,042, and 3 ranks is 0.06% of a 5,452-model panel. Kendall τ is 0.955 and the top ten does not change at all. The criterion fired on two artifacts of scale.

When two benchmarks agree on a ranking, 0.9% of the agreement is shared weighting structure. In denali's own CRISPR work, 26% of the apparent replication between two screens was set size.

Track B · 1,272 published screens × 6 corrections

Which correction to use — and it is not ours

The literature has proposed fixes for this confound since 2012. Nobody had measured how much of the size dependence each actually removes on real data at scale, or how often one makes things worse. The pipeline first had to reproduce the published corpus numbers, and does, on 1,272 of 1,272 screens.

Correction Size dependence removed Screens made worse Verdict
CAMERA, variance-inflated (ρ̄ = 0.05) 85%1.8% Use this one
denali's own residualisation 86%6.3% Unreliable tail
CAMERA, variance-inflated (ρ̄ = 0.01) 62%1.5% Works
ORA hypergeometric — the field's default 57%0.7% Works
Size-preserving permutation null 23%13.4% Partial
Competitive score test, no inflation (ρ̄ = 0) 3%18.2% Fails
The result we pre-committed to reporting

denali's own correction has the best median — and breaks 6.3% of screens against CAMERA-VIF's 1.8%, nearly four times the failure rate for one percentage point of gain. So the tool should recommend CAMERA's method, not its own. An invariant now fails the build if that sentence disappears.

The last two rows are the same test with and without the variance-inflation term. Adding it turns the worst method in the table into the best-behaved one. That is Wu & Smyth's entire 2012 argument, reproduced across a thousand screens run without reference to it: the inflation term is the correction; the competitive framing alone is not.

And the caveat that bounds all of it

Driving a size correlation to zero proves size-decoupling, not correctness. A correction that deleted all biology would score perfectly on every metric here. There is no ground truth in this corpus.

One practical finding for anyone holding a hit list: a correction changes the size dependence far more than it changes who is at the top — except in the most confounded fifth of screens, where applying CAMERA-VIF replaces seven of your top ten.

The row that kills the strongest objection

The standing argument against this work has always been that the confound is an artifact of sloppy curation. Saccharomyces cerevisiae is the best-annotated organism in biology. Across 15.8 million measured gene pairs scored against SGD's curated GO Slim, it lands at the 96th percentile of the published CRISPR corpus — against a pre-registered expectation of merely "at or above the 25th". Five of the top ten terms leave the top ten once size is removed. All four registered sensitivities agree, and normalising for how many pairs each gene was actually tested in makes the effect stronger, not weaker.

The cleanest demonstration

Region sets share nothing with gene-set machinery — no gene symbols, no annotation, no curation. A set is a pile of genomic intervals a ChIP experiment called; its size is antibody, sequencing depth and peak caller. Across 300 experiments spanning a 5,639-fold size range, size alone explains 89% of the ranking and none of the top ten survives re-ranking. An experiment that calls more peaks covers more genome and overlaps more query SNPs. That is area, not biology.

Three negatives inside Track C, reported not buried
  • Two registered hit rules were degenerate — 75% of proteins and 86% of metabolites called hits, which makes hits ≈ rate × size by arithmetic identity. Both flattering numbers (R² 0.70 and 0.91) are printed beside the usable variants and used for nothing. The protein guard was pre-registered and fired on its own.
  • Six of eleven microbiome cohorts return no significant result at all, so their R² does not exist. They are counted as unscoreable, never scored — calling them "not size-dominated" would be exactly the error this project exists to catch.
  • The metabolite boundary failed in the opposite direction from the one anticipated. Small sets are not immune (R² 0.331); a 63-compound assay simply cannot define a meaningful null against sets of 3–8 members.

Because the microbiome domain spans cohorts, it answers denali's replication question directly: across all ten cohort pairs, 28% of cross-cohort agreement is set size — against the 26% measured between two CRISPR cell lines, in a field sharing no organism, assay or annotation with it.

Limits

What this does not say

  • No individual set, model, screen, cohort or publication is named as a finding. The unit of inference is the distribution, throughout.
  • A high R² does not mean any particular set is wrong, and driving one to zero would not make a ranking right.
  • These are six constructions, not six fields. Another defensible construction in the same domain could land elsewhere; the claim is that the effect appears at all outside gene sets.
  • The AI-benchmark result covers one archived leaderboard's models on one benchmark family, scored by one harness. The count-layer figure must never be quoted as "benchmarks are 60% confounded" — nobody ranks on counts.
  • One domain deviates from its registration: the MetaCyc class hierarchy is not publicly obtainable, so the substitute construction was fixed and committed before any value in that domain existed, and is labelled wherever reported.

Each arm carries a gate that must pass before any result is read: Track A recovers item counts from each model's own reported standard error and matches the dataset on 57 of 57 subjects; Track B reproduces the published corpus median on every one of 1,272 screens. Fifty invariants pin the pre-registrations by hash and refuse to let a degenerate hit rule become a headline.
